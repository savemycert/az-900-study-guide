# AZ-900 Exam-Day Guide

This page covers the logistics of the Microsoft Azure Fundamentals (AZ-900) exam: booking, delivery options, check-in, exam format, scoring, and what happens afterward. Every fact links to the official Microsoft Learn page it came from. Policies change, so confirm anything time-sensitive on the linked page before your appointment.

> Also see the [AZ-900 practice questions](https://www.savemycert.com/practice/azure-fundamentals/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=az-900-study-guide) and [timed mock exams](https://www.savemycert.com/mocks/azure-fundamentals/?utm_source=github&utm_medium=readme&utm_campaign=az-900-study-guide).

## Booking the exam

On the Azure Fundamentals certification page, choose **Schedule exam** under Pearson VUE. You sign in to your Learn profile, confirm your legal name (it must match your ID), and add contact details before Pearson VUE takes over. Students and educators can schedule through Certiport instead. Microsoft strongly recommends a personal Microsoft account (MSA): exam records tied to a work or school account are lost if you leave that organization.

You can book up to 90 days ahead and hold at most two Microsoft exam appointments at a time through Pearson VUE. Reschedule or cancel from your Learn profile at least 24 hours before the appointment, or your fee is forfeited. Microsoft does not refund failed exams or missed appointments.

The price depends on the country or region where you test, and taxes may apply, so check the certification page for your local price. The exam is offered in 13 languages, listed on the same page.

Source: [Azure Fundamentals certification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-fundamentals/), [Register and schedule an exam](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/register-schedule-exam), [Reschedule and cancellation policies](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/reschedule-and-cancellation-policy), [Exam FAQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/frequently-asked-questions)

## Online (OnVUE) vs test center

In most cases you can test online or at a Pearson VUE test center. A test center is a quiet, pre-configured room with no software to install. Online, you are responsible for your computer and room meeting the security rules:

- Run Pearson VUE's system test on the same computer, network, and location, before booking and again on exam day. Prefer a personal computer, since work machines often block the OnVUE software.
- Use a walled room with a closed door. No one else may enter during the exam.
- Clear the desk of books, notes, and pens, unplug extra monitors, and turn off other computers.
- Keep your phone out of arm's reach but close enough to hear, in case the proctor calls. A drink is allowed in a clear, spill-proof container.
- Greeters and proctors communicate in English, even if your exam is in another language.

Source: [Register and schedule an exam](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/register-schedule-exam), [About online exams with Pearson VUE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams)

## ID and check-in

You need a current government-issued ID, and the name on it must match the name on your Microsoft Certification profile exactly. Pearson VUE's ID policy lists acceptable documents.

For an online exam, check in from your Learn profile between 30 minutes before and 15 minutes after your appointment. You photograph your face, ID, and exam area with your phone, and a greeter reviews them before placing you in the proctor queue. Check-in takes about 15 minutes.

Source: [About online exams with Pearson VUE](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/online-exams)

## How the exam is structured

You have 45 minutes to answer the questions. For Fundamentals exams, Microsoft suggests allowing 65 minutes of seat time for instructions, the candidate agreement, and optional comments. Most Microsoft exams have 40 to 60 questions, but the number varies.

Microsoft does not say in advance which question types a given exam uses. The free [exam sandbox](https://aka.ms/examdemo) lets you try the types it may use, including multiple choice, drag and drop, build list, hot area, active screen, and case studies. Unlike associate exams, Fundamentals exams do not give you access to Microsoft Learn during the test.

Five minutes are built in for unscheduled breaks. The clock keeps running during a break, and afterward you cannot return to questions you have already seen.

The skills measured are Describe cloud concepts (25–30%), Describe Azure architecture and services (35–40%), and Describe Azure management and governance (30–35%).

Source: [Azure Fundamentals certification](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-fundamentals/), [Exam duration and exam experience](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/exam-duration-exam-experience), [AZ-900 study guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/resources/study-guides/az-900)

## How scoring works

Scores are reported on a scale of 1 to 1,000, and you need 700 or more to pass. Because the score is scaled, 700 does not necessarily mean 70% of the points.

Multi-part questions usually give one point per correct part, so partial credit is possible. There is no penalty for guessing. Some questions are unscored, and you cannot tell which ones, so answer every question.

Source: [Exam scoring and score reports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports)

## Results and score report

For most exams, you see your result within minutes of finishing. The score report shows your overall score, pass or fail, and a bar chart of your performance by skill area. The bars show strengths and weaknesses, but you cannot use them to work out how many questions you got right. Microsoft does not tell you which questions you missed. Your result appears in your Learn profile within 24 hours.

Source: [Exam scoring and score reports](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exam-scoring-reports)

## Your credential

When you pass, the certification appears in your Learn profile under Credentials with an Online Verifiable tag. From there you can share it on LinkedIn or by link and download a certificate. Others can verify it only while your profile is public.

Fundamentals certifications do not expire, so there is no renewal for AZ-900.

Source: [Sharing and validating of Microsoft Credentials](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/cred-share-validate), [Microsoft Certification Renewal](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/renew-your-microsoft-certification)

## If you don't pass

- After a first failed attempt, wait 24 hours before retaking.
- After that, wait 14 days between attempts.
- You can take the same exam at most five times in 12 months, counted from your first attempt.
- You pay for each retake.

Source: [Exam retake policy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/support/retake-policy)

## On exam day: practical tips

- **Try the sandbox first.** It uses the real exam's interface and navigation.
- **Watch the clock.** 45 minutes is short. Mark hard questions for review and keep moving.
- **Answer before a break.** Anything you have seen before a break is locked afterward.
- **Never leave a blank.** A wrong answer costs nothing.
- **Need more time for language?** If the exam is not in your preferred language, you can request an extra 30 minutes. Request it in advance of your exam.
